Essay · I

On Material Integrity in Software

byArnav · Written at the bench, 2026

Most software today is not built. It is assembled. And what is assembled cheaply is made to be thrown away.

A webpage wrapped in a window. A window wrapped in a subscription. A subscription wrapped in a funnel. It arrives weighing hundreds of megabytes to perform the simplest task. It interrupts your work with popups, countdowns, and artificial urgency. It measures how long you linger, and learns, with great patience, how to make you linger longer.

We regard this as a failure of material. A chair of particleboard will hold weight for a season; a chair joined from solid oak is still a chair a century on. Software has materials too. The cheapest of them are bloat, surveillance, and noise. They are everywhere today simply because they are cheap.

Every instrument from this atelier is written natively in Swift for Apple hardware, with the grain of the system rather than against it. Whether on the desk, in the hand, or on the wrist, it opens before you have finished deciding to open it. Its behaviour is governed by invariants: conditions reasoned through before a single line ships, so that the instrument does precisely what it claims, and nothing it does not.

Our tools are built to serve their owner, never to monetize them. There is no advertising machinery, no attention harvesting, and no quiet scheme to trade on your habits. What you do with an instrument is yours in the oldest sense of the word: private, unobserved, and complete.

We make few things, and we make them slowly. We would rather make one tool you keep for a decade than ten you forget by spring.

  1. i.

    Native

    Written for the machine it runs on. Never a webpage in disguise.

  2. ii.

    Invariant

    Correct in every state, not merely in most of them.

  3. iii.

    Private

    Nothing observed. Nothing collected. Nothing transmitted.

  4. iv.

    Owned

    Purchased once, and kept. A tool, not a tenancy.

Instrument No. 01 · Available

Vow

The uncompromised offline lock for macOS.

Vow is not a screen-time toy. There are no streaks to protect, no badges to collect, and no cheerful reminders that you have done well today. It is an instrument of will.

Set the duration. Engage the lock. Vow severs network connections at the level of the system itself, not in a browser extension, and not through a blocklist one clever search can outrun. There is no instant escape, because an instant escape is the whole problem.

You make a vow. The machine holds you to it.
